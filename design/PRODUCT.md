# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Ingmar — a single user, using his parent's car and splitting costs with them. He uses the app on mobile, in the moment (logging a trip right after arriving, checking the balance before paying). Context is brief, glanceable interactions throughout the day, not extended sessions. Three equal jobs: staying in control of what he owes, reducing friction when logging, and proving fairness to the car owner.

## Product Purpose

Vroom tracks car trips, fuel refills, and the running cost balance between Ingmar and the car owner. The core output is a live saldo: who owes what, at a glance. Success means Ingmar never has to manually calculate anything, and every refill or trip takes under 10 seconds to log.

## Brand Personality

Playful · Bold · Dutch. Direct, confident, a bit irreverent. Not a productivity tool trying to be serious — more like a well-made personal object with character. Dutch directness: no fluff, no motivational language, no decorative complexity.

## Anti-references

- **Finance / banking apps** (ING, ABN AMRO, Revolut): sterile, conservative, cold. Accessibility-first grids with zero personality.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards**: navy + teal, hero metrics with big numbers, identical card grids, the same gradient accent every time. This app should not look like it was made from a SaaS template.

## Design Principles

1. **Fast over complete.** Every logging flow should be 2–3 taps max. The app earns trust by getting out of the way.
2. **The saldo is the hero.** Everything else is supporting data. Hierarchy should reflect this on every screen.
3. **Personal, not corporate.** One user, one car. The interface can have character, opinion, and Dutch bluntness.
4. **Playful without infantilizing.** Personality lives in motion, typography, and moments — not badges, streaks, or confetti.
5. **Spatial clarity.** Dense information is fine, but every element needs room to breathe. Rhythm over repetition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Personal use only. No formal compliance requirements. Standard contrast and tap targets are good practice but not a hard constraint.
